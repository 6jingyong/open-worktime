For researchers, NGOs, worker surveys & labor programs

Turn working-time evidence into something people can actually see.

Open Worktime is an open public-interest project for one simple question: how many hours do the people who make our products actually work?

We are building a transparent, privacy-minimal way to connect existing labor research with factories, regions and consumer-facing brands—without asking data partners to rebuild their data systems.

The gap

Where is known. Hours often are not.

Supply-chain datasets increasingly tell us where products are made. Labor studies often measure working conditions. But consumers rarely get a simple, comparable view of actual weekly working-time distributions behind products and brands.

The idea

Publish distributions, not moral scores.

Open Worktime keeps the underlying evidence visible: hour buckets, sample sizes, employment relationships, geography, methodology and provenance. We do not reduce a factory or brand to a single “good/bad” score.

The promise

Do not make the partner do the integration.

Partners can keep their existing survey structure. We take responsibility for source-specific adapters, field mapping, hour normalization, entity resolution and public-facing presentation.

How to collaborate

Three useful levels—from almost zero effort to full research reuse.

1 · Metadata only

Tell us what datasets exist, what the working-time question means, and whether factory/site identifiers are stable. A data dictionary or questionnaire is already useful.

Partner effort: very low
2 · Safe aggregates

Provide factory/site-level hour buckets and sample counts. Raw worker-level records never need to leave your organization.

Partner effort: low
3 · De-identified microdata

Where permitted, provide worker-level weekly hours plus a stable anonymous factory/site ID and survey round. We do not need names or contact details.

What Open Worktime contributes

The partner provides evidence. We do the productization work.

Preserve research meaning

A “typical week”, an annual mean and a four-week average remain different measures. We do not silently force incompatible studies into one statistic.

Resolve entities

We map source-specific factory IDs and legal entities to public facility records and brands only when there is auditable evidence.

Protect privacy

Names, employee IDs, phone numbers, emails, exact salaries and precise private locations are not part of the public observation model.

Publish provenance

Every external import keeps source, license/permission status, transformation rules and comparability notes.

Make it understandable

Consumers see distributions and sample sizes rather than opaque ESG scores or hidden model outputs.

Keep corrections possible

Raw evidence stays separate from later entity mappings and release-time weights, so improved supply-chain knowledge can update analysis without rewriting history.

A simple example

What happens to one existing research dataset?

  1. You already have: anonymous factory ID + survey round + weekly hours.
  2. We build: a documented adapter that preserves the survey's original definition.
  3. We resolve: the factory/site to public facility and supply-chain evidence where possible.
  4. We publish: only permitted aggregates—for example a 10-bin weekly-hours histogram, sample size, geography and provenance.
  5. The public can compare: regions, employment relationships and eventually brands, without seeing individual workers.

If raw microdata cannot be shared, steps 1–2 can stay inside the partner organization and Open Worktime can work from a safe aggregate export instead.

Privacy & governance

We would rather publish less than expose a worker.

Not needed

Name, personal email, phone, employee ID, exact salary, home address, GPS, exact private job title or complaint narrative.

Raw data can remain restricted

A collaboration does not require public release of partner microdata. Permission can cover only derived aggregate histograms and sample counts.

Evidence is not accusation

Different working-time distributions can reflect law, product mix, production cycle, employment structure or management practice. Open Worktime presents the evidence before interpreting causes.

The smallest next step

You do not need to commit to a data transfer.

Start by showing us a questionnaire, a data dictionary, a sample of column names, or an existing public report. We can tell you what is usable, what would remain private, and what public output could be produced before any data changes hands.
